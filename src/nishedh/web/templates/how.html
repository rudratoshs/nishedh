How Nishedh works

India has clear rules about what can be sold online. Pesticides must be registered and properly labelled. Some radio equipment can't be sold online at all. But online stores list millions of products, and sellers write their own descriptions. Nishedh checks those descriptions against the government's own lists, the way an inspector would, and shows its working.

1FindSerpApi searches Google Shopping, Amazon.in and Google (for Flipkart and JioMart) for each product type.
2ReadFor each product we pick out the chemical name, its strength, the radio frequency and any approval number, from the title and the full product page.
3CheckWe compare those details with official government lists and rules, such as the registered pesticide list.
4ExplainEvery result shows the exact words that caused it, the rule, and a link to the official source.

The photo check (Google Lens)

Some listings hide the chemical name. For those, Nishedh sends the product photo to Google Lens through SerpApi, and looks at what visually matching photos are called on other websites, and checks those names against a watchlist of products named in regulator orders. That is how a nameless "5% Active Formula" weed killer turned out to be sold elsewhere as Cyclosinone, the product the regulator fined Amazon, Flipkart and JioMart for in September 2026.

The pesticide lists are the government's own PDF files, read by Nishedh. Each file's fingerprint (SHA-256) is recorded, so anyone can confirm which version was used.

Why SerpApi?

Flipkart and JioMart have no public product API, and Amazon.in blocks automated visitors. SerpApi gives Nishedh reliable, structured search results from Google Shopping, Amazon, Google Search and Google Lens. Every response is saved, so each result can be traced back to the exact data it came from.
